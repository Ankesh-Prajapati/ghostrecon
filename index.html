GOOGLE DORKING FAQ

What is Google dorking?

Google dorking (also called Google hacking) is the practice of using advanced search operators — like site:, filetype:, and inurl: — to find information that isn't easily reachable through a normal search. Security researchers use dorks during OSINT and reconnaissance to surface exposed files, login pages, and misconfigured services on the public web. Try GhostRecon's dork library →

Is Google dorking illegal?

No — running a search query is not illegal. Dorking only surfaces links to publicly indexed pages; it doesn't access, download, or modify anything. What you do with the results can be illegal (e.g. accessing a system without authorization), so always dork within the scope of an authorized bug bounty program, VAPT engagement, or your own assets.

What is GhostRecon?

GhostRecon is a free, browser-based Google Dorking and OSINT engine with 400+ ready-made dork categories (credential leaks, exposed configs, CVE patterns, admin panels, and more), plus a custom dork constructor, Shodan/Censys/GitHub query builders, search history, and favorites — no signup, no backend, no data leaves your browser.

What's the difference between Google dorking and OSINT?

Google dorking is one technique within OSINT (Open Source Intelligence). OSINT is the broader practice of gathering information from publicly available sources — dorking specifically uses search engine operators to do that against indexed web content.

Where is the Google Hacking Database (GHDB)?

The GHDB is a well-known public archive of Google dorks maintained by Exploit-DB. GhostRecon takes a similar idea but organizes dorks into browsable categories with a live query preview, bulk launch queue, and multi-engine support (Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Brave, Yandex) instead of a flat list.

Yes — GhostRecon's dorks are built with bug bounty and VAPT recon in mind: attack surface mapping, subdomain discovery, exposed credentials, admin panel discovery, and API reconnaissance are all covered. Always stay within the target's authorized scope. Start a recon session →
